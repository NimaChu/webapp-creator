# HTML Presentations

Treat a presentation as a narrative format, not a theme. Derive its visual language from subject, audience, venue, and argument.

## Narrative

- Give each slide one job.
- Establish context before detail.
- Use evidence to support a conclusion, not to fill space.
- End with a decision, implication, or next action.
- Prefer a shorter coherent story over many weak slides.

## Runtime

Support:

- Previous and next controls.
- Arrow, Page Up/Down, Home, and End keys.
- Direct slide hashes when practical.
- Visible slide count or progress.
- One slide per printed page.
- Touch controls when presenting from a phone or tablet.

Add speaker notes and language switching only when the request needs them.

## Layout

Design for projection and distance. Use large type, strong contrast, stable alignment, and limited text. Derive compositions from content rather than repeating one card grid. Keep critical information inside safe edges at narrow and wide aspect ratios.

## Validation

Test every slide, first and last boundaries, direct hashes, keyboard controls, narrow screens, reduced motion, fullscreen fallback when present, and print/PDF output.
